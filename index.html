Neighborhood Context: Exposition Park is Dallas’ historic streetcar suburb hub. The DART Green Line tracks along Parry Avenue follow the exact route of the original 1890s electric streetcars that ferried fairgoers to the front gates.

Walk 300 feet to Parry Ave Front Gate; scan digital passes at 10:00 AM. Walk straight across the Esplanade into the Automobile Building & Centennial Building for 60 minutes of uninterrupted, empty-hall air conditioning.
Auto Show History: The Texas Auto Show has operated inside the State Fair since 1913, making it older than the Detroit Auto Show. In 1936, the Centennial Building housed Chrysler’s futuristic exhibits, while the Automobile Building burned down in 1942 and was rebuilt in 1948 with Pierre Bourdelle’s murals celebrating Texas oil wildcatters.

Big Tex Morning Photo & 11:30 AM Pig Races

Big Tex Trivia: Big Tex debuted in 1952. He was originally built in 1949 as a giant paper-mâché Santa Claus in the small town of Kerens, Texas, before Dallas fair directors bought his 52-foot steel frame for $750 and added his iconic cowboy hat and vocal drawl.

Old Mill History: Built for the 1936 Texas Centennial Exposition as the Morten Milling Industry Building, this rough-stone structure originally operated as a real working experimental flour mill with full-scale grain milling machinery inside.

Butter Sculpture Lore: Begun in the 1990s inside a custom glass-walled refrigerated booth held at 38°F, the sculptor spends over 150 hours hand-carving more than 1,000 pounds of Texas salted butter. This year’s sculpture celebrates both the Fair's 140th anniversary and America's 250th birthday.

Hall of State Architecture: Completed in 1936 at a cost of $1.2 million, it was the crowning jewel of the Texas Centennial. The bronze statue outside is the gold-leafed Tejas Warrior by Allie Victoria Tennant, while Eugene Savage's murals inside were the largest continuous historical indoor murals in the world when unveiled.

Interactive Auto Show Thrill Rides

  • Camp Jeep (Centennial Building): Indoor off-road ride-along where professional drivers tackle a 28-foot 35-degree mountain climb, timber logs, and 30-degree side wedges testing Wrangler Rubicon and Gladiator 4xe articulation in freezing A/C.
  • Ram 1500 RHO Thrill Ride (Gate 5 Track): High-speed outdoor acceleration course demonstrating the 540-hp twin-turbo Hurricane engine over dirt rollers and high-banked curves.
  • Chevrolet Ride & Drive (Chevy Park Plaza): Complimentary test-drive course (10 AM – 7 PM). Licensed drivers over 18 can get behind the wheel of a Silverado HD, Colorado, or Corvette Grand Sport.
  • Kids Drive Zone (Centennial Building): Safe indoor mini-track where children pilot battery-powered electric versions of Ram trucks and Jeep Wranglers.

Auto Show History & Architectural Context

  • 113-Year Tradition: Founded in 1913, the Texas Auto Show inside the State Fair predates almost all major metropolitan auto shows, reflecting the sheer purchasing power of Lone Star drivers.
  • 1936 Exposition Halls: The Centennial Building originally showcased Art Deco styling for Chrysler’s luxury models. Look up at the soaring ceiling trusses designed to permit massive clear-span exhibit floors.
  • Bourdelle Frescoes: The Automobile Building's exterior features relief sculptures and murals created by Pierre Bourdelle during the post-WWII reconstruction, celebrating locomotives, turbines, and drilling rigs.
