Today's Walking Timeline Morning To 6 PM
8:48 – 9:48 AM 816 S 2nd → Exposition
Parking Systems Lot & Noble Coyote Coffee
Parked at Parking Systems of America ($40 paid). Walk 0.3 miles north to arrive at Noble Coyote Coffee Roasters (819 Exposition Ave) at 9:30 AM sharp. Enjoy pour-overs, iced espresso, and scratch pastries before fair crowds form.
Neighborhood Context: Exposition Park is Dallas’ historic streetcar suburb hub. The DART Green Line tracks along Parry Avenue follow the exact route of the original 1890s electric streetcars that ferried fairgoers to the front gates.
9:48 – 11:00 AM Front Gate → Auto Building
10:00 AM Gate Rope Drop & Texas Auto Show (60 Minutes)
Walk 300 feet to Parry Ave Front Gate; scan digital passes at 10:00 AM. Walk straight across the Esplanade into the Automobile Building & Centennial Building for 60 minutes of uninterrupted, empty-hall air conditioning.
Auto Show History: The Texas Auto Show has operated inside the State Fair since 1913, making it older than the Detroit Auto Show. In 1936, the Centennial Building housed Chrysler’s futuristic exhibits, while the Automobile Building burned down in 1942 and was rebuilt in 1948 with Pierre Bourdelle’s murals celebrating Texas oil wildcatters.
11:00 – 11:45 AM Big Tex Circle → Chevy Plaza
Big Tex Morning Photo & 11:30 AM Pig Races
Snap opening morning photos in front of Big Tex before the crowds pack the circle, load food/ride coupons at kiosks, and walk to Chevrolet Park Plaza for the 11:30 AM Statewide Remodeling Pig Races.
Big Tex Trivia: Big Tex debuted in 1952. He was originally built in 1949 as a giant paper-mâché Santa Claus in the small town of Kerens, Texas, before Dallas fair directors bought his 52-foot steel frame for $750 and added his iconic cowboy hat and vocal drawl.
11:45 AM – 12:45 PM Grand Ave → Old Mill
Opening Day Parade (12:00 PM) at Rousso's Wine Garden
Claim a shaded outdoor patio table at Rousso’s Wine Garden at The Old Mill on Grand Ave. Order Texas reserve wine, a frozen wine cocktail, or a cold ZiegenBock. Watch the marching bands and floats roll right past your table.
Old Mill History: Built for the 1936 Texas Centennial Exposition as the Morten Milling Industry Building, this rough-stone structure originally operated as a real working experimental flour mill with full-scale grain milling machinery inside.
12:45 – 2:00 PM Tower Food → Creative Arts
Tower Lunch, The Butter Sculpture & Cooking Demos
Enjoy an air-conditioned lunch inside the Tower Building (Fletcher’s Original Corny Dog & Big Tex Choice winners). Walk into Creative Arts to view the refrigerated Stars, Stripes, and Howdies Butter Sculpture and the 1:30 PM Cutco Kitchen cooking demo.
Butter Sculpture Lore: Begun in the 1990s inside a custom glass-walled refrigerated booth held at 38°F, the sculptor spends over 150 hours hand-carving more than 1,000 pounds of Texas salted butter. This year’s sculpture celebrates both the Fair's 140th anniversary and America's 250th birthday.
2:00 – 3:45 PM Hall of State → Go Texan
Hall of State Centennial Murals & GO TEXAN Tastings
Escape peak solar heat inside the limestone-encased Hall of State. Walk the Great Hall to view 3-story murals and Mundo Latino: Día de los Muertos. Continue into the GO TEXAN Pavilion for free food samples, chocolate milk, and a cold beer tasting.
Hall of State Architecture: Completed in 1936 at a cost of $1.2 million, it was the crowning jewel of the Texas Centennial. The bronze statue outside is the gold-leafed Tejas Warrior by Allie Victoria Tennant, while Eugene Savage's murals inside were the largest continuous historical indoor murals in the world when unveiled.
3:45 – 5:00 PM Marine Corps Sq → 816 S 2nd
All-Star Stunt Dog Show (4:00 PM) & Walk to Car
Catch the 4:00 PM Stunt Dog Show at Marine Corps Square as the sun angle drops to ~34°, eliminating direct crown solar radiation. Walk through Gate 3 (Grand Ave) down 2nd Ave back to your parked car at 816 S 2nd Ave by 5:00 PM.
Woofus Myth: On your walk toward Marine Corps Square, spot The Woofus at United Ag Plaza—a 1936 Art Deco statue with the head of a sheep, mane of a horse, wings of a duck, tail of a turkey, body of a pig, and horns of a Texas Longhorn.
5:05 – 5:55 PM I-30 West → Pantego
Drive Home via I-30 West
Pull out onto 2nd Ave, merge onto I-30 West toward Fort Worth/Arlington, and drive back to 2806 Sarah Drive in Pantego, arriving right around 6:00 PM.

Centennial & Auto Porticos Colonnades flanking pool 10:00 – 11:00 AM Murals by Carlo Ciampaglia (Centennial) and Pierre Bourdelle (Auto Building) depicting trains, planes, and rocketry guided by angels. Photo Tip: Long perspective shot walking down the shaded porticos.
Tejas Warrior & Hall of State Terminus of Esplanade 2:00 – 3:00 PM Allie Victoria Tennant's gold-leaf warrior holding a bow. Photo Tip: Shoot from the limestone steps to catch the golden figure reflecting sunlight, then step inside the Great Hall for wide-angle ceiling shots.
The Woofus Beast United Ag & Turf Plaza 11:20 AM Whimsical Art Deco mythological beast combining all major Texas livestock. Photo Tip: Capture a side profile showing the duck wings and massive Longhorns simultaneously.
Leonhardt Lagoon Sculptures Leonhardt Lagoon 10:45 AM or 3:30 PM Patricia Johanson's 1986 organic terra cotta bridges (Delta Duck-Potato & Texas Fern). Photo Tip: Stand at the bridge curves looking over the water with weeping willows in the background.
